# Verdict: **NOT SATISFIED**

**What decided it:** nobody ever strikes the shuttle — the racket is never in a hand and never swings (it floats fixed against the near player's shoulder/chest for all 108 frames), and the player who is supposed to return it has no racket at all and stands motionless with his arms down while the shuttle reverses direction at his chest — so at normal viewing size the shot reads as the shuttle floating across and floating back, not as a serve and a return.

Video checked: 1280×720, 24 fps, 108 frames, 4.50 s, silent. Watched the five delivered stills whole, then sampled at 12, 24 and 25 fps (tiles + full-size `-ss` grabs), plus zooms. Judged at 1:1 viewing size, not at zoom.

## What reads (times where I saw it)

- **The court, the net, the two sides, the camera move.** Net, posts, tram-lines, backdrop and scoreboard are stable and consistent; the wide → medium two-shot → pull-back (0.0–2.6 push in, ~3.4–4.45 pull back to the opening framing) is smooth, no jump, no cut, no black frame. *High confidence.*
- **A shuttle is present, moves from the near player's side to the far player's side and back, and ends on the near player's side.** Positions: on the near court 0.0 → beside the near player's chest 2.0–2.35 → over/behind the net tape at the far player's chest 2.55–2.74 → back across 2.8–3.1 → on the near floor from ~3.25 to the end. So the *sequence of places* is roughly the one intended, and the two "beats" land near the stated 2.3 s and 2.68 s. *High confidence.*
- **The shuttle has a floor shadow and a consistent light**, so it is not a pasted overlay.

## What does not read (frame/second, and what I saw instead)

1. **The racket is not held by anyone, ever — 0.0 s to 4.45 s (all 108 frames).** The head + grip is drawn at a fixed offset from the near player's shoulder/chest and rotates with his torso; his hands are 30–70 px (original scale) away from the grip in every frame I checked (0.00, 0.75, 1.70, 2.30, 2.40, 3.06, 3.60, 4.45). At 1.70–1.80 s the disc sits against his throat/chest. **It never swings, never leaves the torso.** *Very high confidence.*
2. **The serve is never struck — ~1.9–2.4 s.** The shuttle is beside the near player's left hand at ~1.9–2.15 (that moment is readable as "holding it"), then by 2.30 it is a hand's width away and drifting to the net while both his hands stay where they were and the racket stays at his shoulder. A viewer sees a released, levitating shuttle, not a struck serve. *Very high confidence.*
3. **The return is never struck, and the returner has no racket — 2.40–2.90 s.** At 2.60–2.75 the far player is a static figure with both arms hanging at his sides; the shuttle reaches his chest at ~2.66 and reverses by ~2.74 with nothing at it. No arm swing anywhere in 2.4–2.9 (checked every frame at 25 fps). **No hand is visibly at the shuttle at the moment of reversal.** This is the decisive finding. *Very high confidence.*
4. **The serve preparation pose is broken — ~0.9–1.8 s.** The near player folds his torso horizontal at hip height with his head hanging at chest level, legs scissored into an X, the rear foot lifted behind, and the racket disc jammed at his chin; the feet scissor and overlap as he lowers. At normal size it reads as a dive/stumble, not a badminton lunge or low serve. *High confidence (it is a clear pose, the question is only whether it "reads wrong" — it does).*
5. **The shuttle's scale and shape read as a cone, not a shuttle — throughout.** It is ~1/3 of a person's height (~50–60 cm if the player is 1.8 m; real is 6–7 cm) and drawn as a wire-frame cone with a black rim on the open end and a black loop at the cork end. At full size, before I had reasoned about it, I read it as a lampshade/megaphone; the badminton reading only comes from context and from the net/racket nearby. *High confidence about what it looks like; medium confidence about whether that's a deliberate style choice.*
6. **The shuttle is semi-transparent.** The backdrop, the net's white tape and the player's sleeve are visible *through* its feathers (clearest at 2.30–2.45 over the player's chest, and at 2.68 where the top tape reads through the cone). It looks ghosted/holographic whenever it overlaps anything. *Very high confidence.*
7. **The shuttle never tumbles and lands standing on end.** It keeps its feather-up orientation through a horizontal flight, and after descending at ~3.10–3.25 it comes to rest *balanced upright on its cork* on the floor (3.30, 3.60, 3.90, 4.45) with a shadow under it — which is the pose of a placed object, not a landed shuttle. At the end it is standing on the floor beside the net post, with the near player walking past it without looking. *High confidence.*
8. **Dead beat and no reaction — ~3.3–4.45 s.** After the shuttle settles, nothing happens but the camera pull-back: neither player reacts to the landing, the near player stands still with his legs crossed, the far player is motionless. ~1.1 s of no acting at the end of a 4.5 s shot. *High confidence.*
9. **Both players are the same character** (same hair, same blue shirt, same build), and the near player's idle stance has his legs crossed/scissored (clear at 2.15, 2.55, 4.45). For a serve-and-return read, "which one am I following" is muddy; the crossed-legs stance reads as a puppet defect rather than a stance. *Medium-high confidence (a viewer notices the crossed legs; the twin-character issue is a design observation).*
10. **Small joint-gap defects**: arm/shirt holes at the shoulders on both players (visible at 2.45–2.80 as beige notches where the upper arm meets the sleeve). Small at viewing size. *Medium confidence it's noticed; high confidence it's there.*

Explicitly checked and **not** a defect, so I don't over-report: the shuttle passing *behind* the racket grip at ~3.00–3.06 is correct depth (the racket is nearer the camera than the incoming shuttle); the arm crossing in front of the shuttle at 2.35 is ordinary 2D layering; no cut head, no ink over a face, no visible camera jump, no vanished/teleporting shuttle, no net/court geometry contradiction. I also considered and could not confirm a "shuttle slides along the floor after landing" (it moves ~130 px relative to the net post between 3.3 and 4.45, but the concurrent pull-back makes parallax a plausible explanation).

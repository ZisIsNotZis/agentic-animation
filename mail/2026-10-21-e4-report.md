## The authored call an episode writes

```
camera.follow(shuttle)                       // window 1.875 s, zoom cap 1.0
camera.follow(shuttle, {sec: 2.2, zoom: 1.15})
```

`target` must be a live prop or actor handle (it throws otherwise, like `cameraOp`).
`sec` is both the declared window and the block's `durationSec`, so the shot and the
timeline cannot disagree. `zoom` is a cap, not a value: every key is floored at the
stage fit (0.6667 on 1280×720) and widened if the target's own band needs it. The
recipe emits one camera event, `{operation:"follow", target:<id>, z:<cap>}`, and
samples nothing — a recipe cannot see frames that are not its own.

## Limitations

1. **Cost is quadratic in window length**: `cameraKeysFromTracks` re-resolves the whole
   window on every `performanceFrame` call, so a 46-frame window costs ~46 prop
   evaluations per rendered frame; a 30 s follow would be ~720. No caching was added
   (it would need a manifest-keyed memo, which is the node's call).
2. **The head rule is deliberately off for a following shot**: a follow that stepped
   aside for every drawn head lost its own subject (the striker's head band is at the
   stage top, `clearOfHeads` pushed the frame to y 260 and put the shuttle above it). A
   follow can therefore cut a drawn head. Unreviewed by eye.
3. **Bindings read the holder from the drawn frame**: a follow on a carried prop in a
   moving hand is approximate; motor-driven and transform-driven props are exact.
4. **Vertical framing is stage-limited, not subject-centred**: in the air the frame pins
   to y 0, near the ground it travels to y 323, so the prop drifts vertically at the
   extremes. The test asserts its centre stays inside the viewport, not centred.
5. **The compiler path is emulated, not compiled**: no episode names the recipe yet, so
   the test drives the generator against a stub world and applies the compiled
   frame arithmetic by hand. `check`/`make` were not run on an episode.
6. `packages/studio/test/*.test.ts` is not typechecked by either tsconfig.
7. The dense-key window has no upper guard: a very long `sec` writes one key per frame
   with no cap.
